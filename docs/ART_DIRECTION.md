# Chore Raid: Art Direction

Chore Raid is a mobile game that turns household chores into boss fights. The player taps the phone once for every real item they finish (a folded shirt, a washed dish, a thing put away). The boss is literally made of the mess, and it dies exactly when the chore is done.

This file is the style source for all of the game's art, and it is also the source used to create the "Chore Raid" brand in Bloom.

## The look in one sentence

A premium, painterly, stylized fantasy mobile game: chunky readable shapes, rich saturated color, soft rim lighting, set in a moody candle-lit dungeon, with a deadpan sense of humor.

## Style rules

- **Medium:** digital painting with a stylized, slightly 3D look. Chunky proportions, big readable silhouettes, clean edges, painterly texture inside the shapes. Think of a top-grossing fantasy mobile game, not a children's book and not photorealism.
- **Lighting:** a dark scene with a strong warm key light from above, a colored rim light that matches each boss's accent color, and deep soft shadows. Subjects glow slightly against the background.
- **Palette:** a base of deep indigo, violet and cold stone gray (#0d0b1a, #1f1b3d, #3d3670). Each boss owns one hot accent: Laundry Leviathan = violet/magenta, Sink Hydra = cyan/teal, Clutter Golem = amber/orange. Gold (#ffc94d) is reserved for rewards and treasure. Ember orange (#ff6b3d) is for calls to action.
- **Characters:** bosses are menacing but funny. They have big expressive eyes and a smug, deadpan attitude. They are built from real household objects, so you can tell exactly which chore each one is.
- **Composition for sprites:** a single character, centered, full body, facing the viewer, on a plain flat background (so it can be cut out), with nothing cropped at the edges.
- **Composition for arenas:** vertical 9:16 dungeon interiors with a clear empty middle where the boss will stand, darker edges, atmospheric depth (fog, dust, candlelight).
- **Never:** text or letters in the images, logos, watermarks, UI elements, photorealistic people, gore, or a cute/pastel nursery look.

## Tone

Deadpan. The bosses talk like bored, unimpressed bureaucrats of mess. "The Leviathan is unimpressed. Eleven items remain."

## Typography (UI, for reference)

A chunky rounded display face (Lilita One) for titles and numbers, and Inter for body text.

## Asset list

| Asset | Count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Laundry Leviathan | 4 damage stages | A serpent of tangled shirts and socks, sock-puppet eyes, sleeves for fins |
| Sink Hydra | 4 damage stages | Three plate-headed necks rising from soap suds, fork teeth; loses a head per stage |
| Clutter Golem | 4 damage stages | A humanoid of stacked junk: books, a shoe, a mug, tangled cables; chunks fall off as it weakens |
| Mess Elemental | 1 | A neutral gray blob of assorted mess, recolored in-game for custom bosses |
| Arenas | 3 | One 9:16 dungeon background per boss, matching its accent color |
| Treasure chest | 1–2 | Closed chest; glows in the rarity color in-game |
| Hub key art | 1 | All three bosses lurking in a dungeon doorway |
| Trophy icons | a few | Loot such as "Leviathan's Lost Sock" |

Damage stages: 1 = full size and smug, 2 = a bit torn and annoyed, 3 = badly damaged and panicked, 4 = barely holding together and desperate. Stages 2–4 are generated with stage 1 as the reference so it stays the same creature.
